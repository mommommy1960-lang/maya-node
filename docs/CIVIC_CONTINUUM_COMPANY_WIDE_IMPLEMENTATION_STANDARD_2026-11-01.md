# Civic Continuum — Company-Wide Implementation Standard

**Founder-directed company policy and implementation register**  
**Effective: November 1, 2026**  
**Applies to Civic Continuum, Maya Node, SAGE, employees, officers, contractors, vendors, and controlled business processes, subject to applicable law and each party's written scope.**

## 1. Authority and non-negotiable boundaries

The founder has adopted the following company standards. They are not optional aspirations and do not depend on outside permission:

- People over profit. Humanity over hierarchy. Progress over power.
- Human safety, dignity, rights, and access to essential support take priority over quotas, deadlines, revenue targets, reputation, and automated system outputs.
- Technical capability does not confer authority. Every consequential action requires an authorized human decision and a defined scope.
- Truthful reporting, early warning, prevention, cooperation, and verified repair are valued. Concealment, retaliation, manipulation, and repeat preventable harm are not.
- A response after harm is necessary; it does not erase prior warnings, unsafe conditions, or repeated failures.
- No one is punished for good-faith safety reporting, raising a concern, pausing unsafe work, or challenging an inaccurate record.
- No metric, bonus, dashboard, or automated model may reward under-reporting or make a low incident count a substitute for safety.

These standards apply to the company's own conduct first. We do not claim that a policy is technically enforced until the relevant control has been implemented and tested.

## 2. Company-wide application

### Leadership and governance
Leaders must disclose conflicts, document consequential decisions, respond to concerns, preserve evidence, and accept independent scrutiny when their own decisions are implicated. No founder, executive, investor, or high-performing employee is exempt. Good-faith reporting must have a route outside the implicated management chain.

### Hiring, people operations, and working conditions
Hiring and supervision must use fair, job-related criteria. Workload, staffing, training, fatigue, accessibility, and safety risks must be considered before setting production expectations. No person should be pressured to work through a serious hazard or conceal an injury. Employment policies, accommodations, discipline, and investigations must be consistent, documented, and non-retaliatory.

### Compensation and performance incentives
Do not reward low reported incident counts, silence, speed at the expense of safety, or public-relations activity after an incident. Recognize early hazard reporting, stopping unsafe work, worker participation, accurate records, root-cause correction, and independently verified prevention. Review every metric for gaming and harmful side effects before use. Never automate compensation or discipline from incident analytics alone.

### Vendors, contractors, and partners
Use written scopes, access limits, confidentiality and data-handling terms, safety expectations, incident-notification duties, audit/evidence cooperation, subcontractor flow-down requirements, and termination/remediation paths. A vendor's post-incident response does not erase repeated failures. Do not grant access or production authority beyond the approved scope.

### Customer service, billing, and access
Explain prices, limits, renewals, restrictions, and service failures clearly. Provide receipts, human-readable support, appeal and correction paths, fair notice where feasible, proportionate restrictions, and data/work preservation. Do not use customer dependency or financial hardship as leverage. Do not claim support coverage, response times, or restoration capability that is not staffed and tested.

### Product, AI, and technical systems
Use least privilege, scoped permissions, revocation, human approval for consequential actions, audit trails, safe defaults, rollback, testing, and clear failure states. Models and automated systems may assist analysis but cannot establish fault, override rights, or make consequential decisions without authorized human review. Use synthetic or redacted data until live-data handling is specifically approved and demonstrated.

### Data governance and pattern analysis
Collect only data necessary for a defined safety, security, service, or accountability purpose. Define access, retention, correction, deletion, audit, and breach handling before collection. Analyze recurring incidents, near misses, unresolved hazards, ignored warnings, repeat corrective-action failures, delayed responses, record inconsistencies, and possible retaliation. Patterns are investigation signals, not verdicts. Use qualified human review, preserve source evidence, document limitations, and provide a fair way to challenge inaccurate data. Never use safety analytics for unrelated surveillance, protected-class profiling, or retaliation.

### Finance and continuity
Maintain honest financial reporting, monitor runway and obligations, avoid commitments the company cannot support, and communicate service or funding constraints early. Do not conceal insolvency risk, misstate readiness, or promise continuity without a credible operational plan. Protect customer work and records during disputes or service interruptions where safely and lawfully possible.

### Public claims and community impact
Distinguish verified capability from plans, prototypes, assumptions, and aspirations. Do not make unsupported safety, privacy, environmental, health, or social-impact claims. Seek affected-community input before decisions that materially affect them. Publish corrections when public statements prove inaccurate.

## 3. Serious harm and incident response

When a serious injury, death, privacy incident, security event, or other material harm occurs:

1. Protect people and stop or contain ongoing danger. Obtain emergency assistance when needed.
2. Preserve relevant evidence and records; do not alter, delete, or selectively disclose them.
3. Notify affected people and appropriate authorities as required, using compassionate, accurate communication.
4. Support affected workers, families, customers, and coworkers with dignity and without speculation.
5. Arrange impartial investigation when severity, recurrence, dispute, or management involvement warrants it. People whose decisions are under review must not control the investigation.
6. Identify root causes and contributing conditions, including staffing, workload, incentives, equipment, training, supervision, vendors, and ignored warnings.
7. Implement corrective actions with named owners and deadlines; verify effectiveness and recurrence reduction.
8. Communicate lessons and appropriate aggregate findings while protecting private information.
9. Do not close the matter merely because emergency services were called, a statement was published, or a checklist was completed.

This standard does not replace emergency procedures or legal reporting obligations. Actual response contacts and coverage must be staffed and published accurately.

## 5. Required rollout sequence

This is an execution register, not a promise that all controls are already live.

**Phase A — Policy baseline**
- Keep this standard and the Founder Accountability Covenant as controlling company policy.
- Map every existing product, workflow, team function, vendor relationship, and customer promise to the domains above.
- Record each gap, owner, evidence requirement, and target date in GitHub.
- Do not advertise a control as active without a passing test or other suitable evidence.

**Phase B — Minimum operating procedures**
- Create and approve procedures for safety reporting, non-retaliation, serious incidents, customer complaints and appeals, vendor incidents, data requests, security/privacy events, and public corrections.
- Assign actual human owners and backups only with their consent; publish coverage limits honestly.
- Define escalation thresholds and evidence-preservation practices.

**Phase C — Technical controls**
- Inventory data flows and systems before adding monitoring.
- Build least-privilege access, tamper-evident audit events where appropriate, retention/deletion controls, incident intake, recurrence reporting, and human review queues.
- Use synthetic data for development and tests.
- Test false positives, missed events, access abuse, reporting suppression, disparate impact, and attempts to game metrics.
- Keep analytics advisory; no automated guilt, discipline, compensation, or denial of support.

**Phase D — Operational proof**
- Run tabletop exercises and controlled pilots.
- Verify each control against acceptance tests.
- Record failures honestly, assign remediation, and retest.
- Publish only evidence-backed capability statements.

## 6. Decision rights and change control

The founder may establish and strengthen company ethical boundaries. Implementation details that affect workers, customers, personal data, regulated activities, or third parties must also meet applicable law, contractual duties, and safety requirements. Seeking qualified advice is a way to make the company's boundaries enforceable, not a veto over its values.

Changes to production systems, live customer data collection, employee monitoring, payment behavior, external messaging, or consequential automated decisions require a separate, explicit implementation authorization and documented test plan. This document alone does not authorize those actions.

## 7. Accountability test

For every important company decision, ask:

- Who could be harmed, excluded, surveilled, pressured, or left without recourse?
- What incentives does this create, including incentives to hide problems?
- What evidence would reveal a recurring pattern?
- Can affected people report, challenge, and correct the record safely?
- Who has authority to stop the process?
- What proves the correction works and stays working?
- Are we describing reality, or selling an appearance?

**Our standard: build systems where integrity is an asset, prevention is rewarded, accountability is usable, and human dignity is never the price of doing business.**
