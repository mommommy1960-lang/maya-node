# Civic Continuum — Company-Wide Human-First Operating Standards

**Founder-adopted company policy — effective November 1, 2026**  
**Applies across Civic Continuum, Maya Node, SAGE, research, products, services, contractors, vendors, and future business units.**  
**Policy status:** The ethical and management boundaries below are adopted now. A control is not represented as technically deployed, legally certified, staffed, or independently verified unless the evidence register says so.

## 1. Purpose and authority

Civic Continuum exists to make technology and institutions serve people. Our operating model must make prevention, integrity, truthful reporting, repair, and human dignity practical—not financially or professionally self-defeating.

The founder adopts this policy as the company's internal standard. No customer, investor, vendor, manager, deadline, model, quota, or revenue target may authorize a departure from it. The founder is subject to it too.

**Core commitments:** People over profit. Humanity over hierarchy. Progress over power. Technical capability does not confer authority. We will not hide a problem to preserve the appearance of success.

This policy establishes company boundaries. It does not by itself create a legal entity, employment contract, collective bargaining agreement, insurance coverage, regulatory certification, or authority to collect personal data. Applicable law and individual rights remain in force.

## 2. Decision hierarchy

When objectives conflict, decisions must follow this order:

1. Protect life, health, safety, rights, and dignity.
2. Stop or contain ongoing harm and prevent unauthorized actions.
3. Preserve evidence and tell the truth about what is known and unknown.
4. Meet legal duties and honor valid commitments to affected people.
5. Correct root causes and verify that the correction works.
6. Resume work only when the responsible human determines that it is safe and authorized.
7. Consider schedule, cost, growth, and reputation within those boundaries.

No production target, customer deadline, computer-generated score, incentive, or financial result overrides this hierarchy.

## 3. Stop-work and speak-up rights

Every worker and contractor engaged in company work is authorized to raise a concern and request a pause when they reasonably believe an activity may cause serious harm, violate a person's rights, exceed authorization, expose data, or compromise safety or integrity.

- A good-faith stop-work request must not trigger retaliation, loss of earned compensation, threats, humiliation, or adverse treatment.
- The concern must be acknowledged, recorded with only necessary information, assessed by a responsible human, and answered with the reason for resuming or changing the work.
- If the direct manager is implicated, the report must bypass that manager.
- Anonymous or confidential reporting routes should be provided where practical and lawful; do not promise anonymity when the system cannot protect it.
- No one may be required to waive legal rights or remain silent as a condition of receiving support.

Until a staffed reporting channel and backup are actually established, the company must disclose the coverage gap. A mailbox or automated alert is not continuous human monitoring.

## 4. Workforce, hiring, and working conditions

Civic Continuum will design work so that people can perform it safely and sustainably.

- Hiring and advancement decisions must use job-related criteria, accessible processes, and consistent documentation. No discrimination, harassment, coercion, or retaliation.
- Role expectations, authority, pay basis, working hours, overtime treatment, benefits if any, and reporting routes must be clear before work begins.
- Staffing plans must account for realistic workload, training, breaks, fatigue, accessibility, and backup coverage. Chronic understaffing is a risk to be addressed, not a reason to normalize unsafe work.
- No employee or contractor may be pressured to falsify records, skip safety steps, conceal a defect, work through dangerous fatigue, or misrepresent product readiness.
- Performance management must consider quality, safe conduct, teamwork, learning, and verified outcomes—not volume alone.
- Good-faith reporting, near-miss disclosure, escalation, and stopping unsafe work must not lower a person's performance rating.
- Employment decisions and compensation must comply with applicable wage, hour, leave, accommodation, safety, and anti-discrimination requirements.

Civic Continuum will not claim that a formal HR program, benefits package, or staffed safety function exists until it is actually established.

## 5. Incentives and compensation

The company will reward behaviors that reduce risk and create durable value. Examples include identifying a hazard early, stopping unsafe work, accurate incident reporting, helping affected people, correcting root causes, and demonstrating that a fix remains effective.

The company will not:
- pay bonuses merely for low injury, complaint, incident, or report counts;
- penalize a team because it reported more near misses or surfaced previously hidden risks;
- reward a manager for closing cases quickly without checking the quality of the remedy;
- treat an emergency call, public statement, apology, or press release as proof that the underlying risk was controlled;
- reward concealment, retaliation, record manipulation, unsafe speed, or shifting risk to another team or community.

Any incentive plan must be reviewed for unintended effects before launch. Use balanced measures: reporting access and trust, time to contain risk, overdue hazards, repeat events, quality of corrective actions, worker participation, and independently or separately verified effectiveness. No single metric should determine pay or discipline. Do not automate compensation, discipline, hiring, or termination decisions from analytics.

Where a financial reward is not affordable or appropriate, recognition, paid time to improve a process, training, and authority to fix the issue may still reinforce the right behavior. Do not promise a bonus that has not been funded and approved.

## 6. Customer and community treatment

Customers and affected communities must receive honest terms, respectful communication, accessible explanations, meaningful correction routes, and proportionate decisions.

- Explain price, renewal, limits, data use, retention, service interruptions, cancellation, and remedies before commitment.
- Distinguish a billing issue, usage limit, security restriction, and technical failure; never invent a reason.
- Give a fair path to correct mistakes, appeal consequential decisions, and reach a responsible human.
- Preserve customer work and data during disputes where safely and lawfully possible; do not use unrelated access as leverage.
- Do not require sensitive information when an essential service can be delivered without it.
- Do not make claims of savings, safety, compliance, accuracy, or readiness beyond measured evidence.
- A customer complaint is a signal to understand and address, not a reason to punish the customer.

The detailed subscription and service-continuity standard remains in [Consumer Trust, Subscription Fairness, and Service Continuity](CONSUMER_TRUST_SUBSCRIPTION_AND_SERVICE_CONTINUITY.md).

## 7. Vendors, partners, and procurement

Partners and vendors must be selected and managed according to competence, safety, security, accessibility, labor practices, transparency, and ability to correct failures—not price or speed alone.

Before access or work begins, define in writing: scope, authority, data access, confidentiality, ownership, subcontracting, security expectations, incident notice, records, audit cooperation, correction duties, exit, and deletion/return of data.

- Apply least privilege and need-to-know access.
- Do not provide credentials or customer data merely to obtain a quote.
- Do not permit unapproved subcontractors to receive protected information.
- Require vendors to report incidents and material control failures promptly under agreed terms.
- Review repeated failures and corrective-action quality; do not reset the relationship's risk history after each contract renewal.
- Suspend new work when a material safety, integrity, privacy, or authorization concern cannot be contained.
- Preserve lawful reporting rights and do not use confidentiality terms to conceal wrongdoing.

The [Partner IP and Manufacturing Boundaries](PARTNER_IP_AND_MANUFACTURING_BOUNDARIES.md) document provides additional intellectual-property limits. It is not a substitute for a signed contract.

## 8. Serious incidents, injury, and death

Human life is never an operating expense. When a serious injury, death, credible threat, major privacy breach, or other significant harm occurs:

1. Protect people first. Call emergency services when appropriate, stop unsafe work, and prevent further exposure.
2. Treat the affected person and family with dignity, compassion, privacy, and respect. Do not reduce a person to a metric or production loss.
3. Preserve relevant evidence and records. Do not alter, delete, backdate, coach witnesses, or speculate publicly.
4. Notify appropriate people and authorities as required by law and agreed procedures. Be accurate about what is confirmed and what remains unknown.
5. Support affected workers, witnesses, family members, and community members without pressuring them to provide statements or waive rights.
6. Arrange an impartial investigation proportionate to the severity and potential conflict. A person whose decisions are under examination must not control the investigation.
7. Identify system causes, including workload, staffing, training, equipment, incentives, ignored warnings, vendor conduct, and management decisions—not only the last person in the chain.
8. Correct the root causes, assign accountable owners and dates, and verify effectiveness before resuming affected work.
9. Communicate appropriate findings and lessons while protecting personal and legally restricted information.

Calling an ambulance, reporting an incident, apologizing, or making a public announcement is necessary in some circumstances but does not erase prior warnings, recurring hazards, or preventable decisions. A response after harm is not a substitute for prevention.

## 9. Pattern detection, records, and anti-gaming

The company will seek to identify recurring harm and failed controls rather than treating each event as isolated.

Potential signals include repeated injuries or near misses; repeated complaints about the same process; hazards left open; corrective actions repeatedly marked complete without durable effect; long delays between warnings and response; unusual missing or altered records; retaliation concerns; and incentive metrics that coincide with reduced reporting or unsafe pressure.

Rules for data use:

- Collect only data necessary for a defined safety, integrity, service-quality, or accountability purpose.
- Document the purpose, data fields, source, access roles, retention period, deletion method, and appeal/correction route before collection.
- Prefer aggregate or de-identified analysis where it is sufficient.
- Restrict sensitive records; log access and changes; protect evidence from unauthorized alteration.
- Keep private personal and customer details out of public repositories, issues, and demonstrations.
- Use trend signals to prioritize human review, not as proof of wrongdoing.
- Do not use analytics to profile protected groups, suppress lawful organizing, retaliate, or conduct unrelated surveillance.
- Do not automate guilt findings, discipline, hiring, firing, compensation, eligibility, or denial of support from a pattern score.
- Give affected people a fair opportunity to correct inaccurate records and provide context.
- Preserve a traceable record of the signal, human review, decision, corrective action, and verification.

A repeat pattern may show that the system is failing even when each individual incident was handled correctly. Conversely, a statistical anomaly may have innocent explanations. Both possibilities must be examined fairly.

## 10. Maya Node and product-development requirements

Maya Node must embody the same boundaries it documents:

- Human authority and purpose-limited permission before consequential actions.
- Clear actor, scope, time, authorization, execution result, and correction ownership.
- Revocation and stop controls that are tested, not merely described.
- Reviewable receipts that distinguish actual events from simulations and do not overstate proof.
- Least-privilege access, account isolation, data minimization, retention, deletion, and recovery tests.
- Human review and appeal routes for consequential decisions.
- Safe failure: uncertain outcomes must be labeled uncertain; mutating actions must not be blindly retried.
- No hidden production changes, live payment activation, or customer-data processing outside explicit authorization and tested readiness gates.
- Product claims must match current evidence. A policy document, local test, synthetic demo, or successful build is not proof of hosted production readiness.

These are product requirements. They are not a claim that every requirement is already implemented in the running software.

## 11. Environmental and community responsibility

For products, procurement, operations, and research, consider material use, energy, emissions, water, waste, repairability, accessibility, and effects on nearby communities. Prefer durable, repairable designs and responsible disposal over artificial replacement cycles.

Do not claim an environmental benefit without a defined baseline, method, boundary, and evidence. Do not shift pollution, risk, or disposal burdens to less powerful communities. Assess foreseeable environmental and community harms before scaling an activity.

## 12. Leadership accountability and conflicts

The founder and senior decision-makers are bound by the same rules as everyone else.

- Disclose material conflicts of interest and related-party benefits.
- Separate approval, execution, and verification where practical.
- Keep decision records for consequential exceptions, incidents, financial commitments, and changes to safeguards.
- Do not retaliate against criticism or good-faith disagreement.
- Seek independent investigation when leadership is implicated or impartiality cannot be maintained internally.
- Correct public statements when evidence changes; preserve the original and explain the correction.
- Never claim outside endorsement, certification, partnership, or approval without evidence.

Until an independent board, ombudsperson, or investigator is actually appointed, do not imply one exists. If the founder is implicated and no independent reviewer is available, pause the affected decision or work and disclose the governance gap rather than self-certifying impartiality.

## 13. Financial stewardship and continuity

Financial discipline must support—not displace—the mission.

- Do not spend money the company does not have or promise benefits that are not funded.
- Track cash runway, obligations, vendor concentration, support capacity, security costs, and recovery costs.
- Diversify critical dependencies where feasible and maintain documented exit and backup plans.
- Do not make safety, privacy, or security optional merely to meet a short-term margin target.
- If the company cannot safely provide a service, pause or narrow it, explain the situation, preserve customer rights and data, and provide a fair transition where possible.
- Do not use customer funds for unrelated purposes or misrepresent the status of payment, billing, or delivery.

## 14. Exceptions, enforcement, and correction

No exception may waive human rights, legal duties, truthful records, or the prohibition on retaliation. Temporary technical exceptions must be documented with reason, scope, owner, duration, risk controls, and review date. If a safeguard cannot be met, pause or narrow the affected activity.

Reports of policy violations must be recorded and assessed without retaliation. Corrective action should address both individual conduct and system conditions. Intentional falsification, retaliation, unauthorized disclosure, or deliberate concealment may result in proportionate action consistent with law and fair process.

## 15. Adoption versus implementation evidence

**Adopted now:** the decision hierarchy; dignity and safety commitments; stop-work and non-retaliation principles; truthful reporting; anti-gaming incentive rules; customer fairness; partner boundaries; incident-response expectations; privacy-conscious pattern-review principles; leadership accountability; and environmental/financial stewardship.

**Not automatically deployed by adoption:** an employee reporting hotline; HR/payroll or bonus program; independent board or investigator; continuous incident monitoring; automated pattern analytics; a production incident-management platform; complete vendor audits; customer appeals staffing; Maya Node production enforcement; or environmental measurement systems.

Before any such system is used, define its owner, budget, lawful basis, access, training, escalation, retention, testing, and evidence of operation. Until then, disclose the gap and do not imply the control exists.

## 16. Review and continuous improvement

Review this policy at least annually and after any serious incident, material product change, significant vendor failure, or evidence that an incentive is producing harmful behavior. Include worker and affected-community input when available. Record changes, reasons, effective dates, unresolved risks, and verification evidence.

**Standard of success:** people can raise concerns safely; hazards are prevented or corrected; repeated failures are recognized; customers retain dignity and meaningful remedies; data is used proportionately; and integrity is rewarded rather than punished.

**Civic Continuum will not ask people to sacrifice their humanity to keep our systems running. We will build systems that allow humanity to flourish.**
