## Shared question

An AI system may be capable of acting without possessing legitimate authority to act. Effective governance therefore requires more than a nominal “human in the loop.” The responsible human must have timely information and real authority to inspect, challenge, pause, refuse, correct, reverse, and escalate consequential action without retaliation.

|---|---|---|

| Continuing review | Hash-linked audit history, incident preservation, correction records, regression tests, and claim downgrading after failure | Public method; operational deployment evidence remains limited |

## Distinct contribution offered for review

The Commons work treats five frequently separated problems as one accountability chain:

1. permission before action;
2. bounded authority during action;
3. preserved evidence after action;
4. a meaningful challenge and correction route for affected people; and
5. consequences for institutions that retain responsibility while denying humans the power to intervene.

The central test is not whether a human appears somewhere in a workflow. It is whether that human has enough information, time, authority, and protection to say **no** effectively.
