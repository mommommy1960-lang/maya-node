## Strategic thesis to test
Civic Continuum's long-term ambition is to become dependable infrastructure people and organizations can rely on across essential needs and industries. The first testable wedge is not a universal marketplace or generic AI assistant. It is a narrow, independent action-assurance capability: make it easier to see what was requested, who authorized it, what an AI-assisted or automated workflow attempted, what the external system actually confirmed, and what needs human review.

This is a hypothesis, not a validated market finding. We must identify one repeated, costly problem before building more.

## Current capability boundary
Maya Node is presently a bounded permission-and-action-record prototype. Discovery can offer a short conversation and, if appropriate, a founder-guided demonstration using invented information only. It is not a production customer service, does not process customer data, does not run live business automation, and does not take payments. No savings, compliance, security, or readiness guarantee is made.

## Discovery method
For each organization:
1. Verify business identity, current status, location, official website or public contact route, and whether it is independent or centrally controlled.
2. Identify a suitable role to invite (owner, office manager, operations lead); do not guess a person's name or email.
3. Ask for a 15-minute exploratory conversation. No sales pressure and no request for confidential records.
4. Ask about one real workflow in general terms: frequency, time spent, error/rework, consequences, current tools, approval authority, and how completion is confirmed.
5. Ask what the business has already tried and why it is insufficient.
6. Ask whether a controlled demonstration with invented information would be useful; no customer data or production access.
7. Record evidence separately from interpretation. Mark unanswered items as unknown.

## Interview questions
- Walk me through one repetitive administrative task from request to completion.
- Where does the process most often stall, get repeated, or go wrong?
- How do you know who approved the action and what was actually completed?
- What does a mistake or missing confirmation cost in time, money, customer trust, or risk?
- How often did this happen in the last month?
- What tools or vendors do you use now? What do they fail to solve?
- Who owns the budget and who would need to approve a change?
- If a solution fixed this reliably, what would make it worth paying for? What budget or existing spend would it replace?
- What would prevent adoption?

## Evidence rubric
For each interview, capture:
- Evidence: direct quote or specific described example (with permission).
- Frequency: per day/week/month; unknown if not stated.
- Impact: time, rework, missed revenue, dispute, risk; quantify only when participant provides a basis.
- Current workaround and named incumbent tools.
- Buyer, user, approver, and budget owner (if known).
- Willingness signal: curiosity, demo request, pilot consent, budget discussion, or explicit willingness to pay. Do not conflate these.
- Disconfirming evidence: low frequency, low consequence, existing tool solves it, no budget, no authority, or no interest.
- Follow-up permission and date.

## Decision gates
Do not declare product-market fit based on polite interest. Continue to prototype only if at least 3 independent businesses describe substantially the same costly workflow problem, explain why current alternatives fail, and identify a credible buyer and measurable value. A paid pilot requires separate explicit agreement and readiness gates. If the interviews show different problems or no willingness to pay, revise or stop the hypothesis.

## Competitive comparison to perform
For every repeated problem, compare current software and manual workarounds on:
- Cross-vendor portability and independence.
- Whether records distinguish requested, authorized, attempted, externally confirmed, failed, and human-reviewed actions.
- Evidence export and retention controls.
- Setup time, total cost, and required technical skill.
- Exception handling and human stop/override.
- Fit for a small business with limited IT staff.

Do not claim that Microsoft, UiPath, Celonis, Zapier, or other incumbents lack governance or audit features without feature-by-feature evidence. The opportunity, if any, must be a specific measurable gap.

## Safety and outreach controls
- No customer PII, financial records, credentials, API keys, production access, live automation, payment, or email dispatch in discovery.
- No contact until the founder approves the exact recipient, channel, and message.
- Never describe the prototype as production-ready, compliant, certified, immutable, or independently validated.
- Record outreach status accurately: not researched, researched, approved, sent, replied, declined, interviewed, or follow-up permitted.
