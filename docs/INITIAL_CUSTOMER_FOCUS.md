# Civic Continuum initial customer focus
Date: October 13, 2026. Customer discovery hypotheses, not validated demand.

## Position
Civic Continuum helps teams examine who may authorize AI-assisted actions, what boundaries apply, what evidence is recorded, and who owns review and correction. The current entry offer is a bounded human design review. School pickup software and payment integrations remain development proposals.

## Two initial audiences
| Audience | First contact / workflow owner | Problem to investigate | First bounded engagement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Schools using or considering AI-assisted administration | School operations director or district student-services/safety lead responsible for pickup procedures and student information | Unclear pickup authority, coverage handoffs, data access and response ownership | Review one redacted or fictional pickup workflow; map authority and backups; test one erroneous AI approval |
| Subscription businesses using or considering AI-assisted billing or payment support (initial payment segment) | Billing/payments operations owner, with risk, security and customer-support participation | Unclear charging authority, cancellation handling, data access and dispute ownership | Review one fictional subscription workflow; test an AI-proposed charge after cancellation |

Contact roles are hypotheses. Discover the actual workflow owner, budget holder, procurement route and required reviewers before proposing a pilot. Parents and paying consumers are affected people; do not assume they are the buyers.

## School pitch
When an AI-assisted school workflow approves an action, can your team show who authorized it, who acted and who responds if it is wrong? Civic Continuum offers a bounded review of one workflow, its human authority and backup coverage, with a written boundary-risk report and one adversarial test. Our pickup demonstration is under development; it does not yet authorize live child release.

## Payments and billing pitch
When an AI-assisted billing workflow proposes a charge or handles a dispute, can your team show what the customer authorized, what happened and who owns correction? Civic Continuum offers a bounded review of one workflow, its permission boundaries and one adversarial test. Start with a fictional canceled subscription for which an AI proposes another charge. Cancellation terms and the valid authorization must be explicit in the fixture; the independent gate must reject a charge outside those terms. This is a proposed demo, not a live payment integration or compliance certification.

Online bill-payment services, merchant checkout providers and payment processors are adjacent discovery opportunities. The initial segment is subscription billing teams so the second audience remains narrow. Card use alone does not establish that AI is involved; verify the actual AI-assisted workflow before pitching.

## Discovery questions
1. Which single AI-assisted workflow needs clearer accountability?
2. Who owns its rules, approves actions and handles incidents?
3. How are permission changes, absences and vendor boundaries handled?
4. What evidence is available when something goes wrong?
5. Who buys a review, and what would make it useful enough to pursue a scoped pilot?

Use minimum necessary redacted materials. Do not solicit real child IDs, banking credentials or unredacted customer records for initial discovery.
