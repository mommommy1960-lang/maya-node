# START HERE — Maya Node Public Due Diligence

**Purpose:** give reviewers one concise, evidence-first path through the parts of The Commons Initiative most relevant to human authority, consent, revocation, auditability, and accountable AI action.

## Executive thesis

Maya Node treats **capability and authority as different things**. An AI system may be technically capable of an action without being authorized to take it.

The architecture therefore separates:

1. identity,
2. requested operation,
3. authorization and permission scope,
4. freshness / expiration,
5. revocation,
6. execution,
7. post-action audit evidence,
8. human challenge / override pathways.

This is not presented as legal certification or a guarantee of safety. It is a technical and governance architecture intended to make authority inspectable and bounded.

## What a reviewer should evaluate

The most useful review questions are:

- Does the architecture preserve a meaningful distinction between what a system **can** do and what it is **allowed** to do?
- Can authority be scoped, expired, revoked, and audited?
- Can a human interrupt, challenge, or inspect consequential behavior?
- Is the authorization trail distinguishable from the model's own reasoning or preferences?
- Are claims clearly separated into demonstrated, proposed, and future work?

## Evidence boundary

The Commons Initiative intentionally distinguishes:

- public research,
- tested software behavior,
- proposed architecture,
- commercial review services,
- future development.

No claim should be read more broadly than the evidence supporting it.
