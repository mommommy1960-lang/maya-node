# Partner IP and Manufacturing Boundaries

**Public policy summary for contractors, engineering partners, manufacturers, and reviewers.**

This document is a policy statement, not a substitute for a signed development or manufacturing agreement and not legal advice.

## 1. Background IP remains with its owner

All inventions, software, source code, algorithms, architectures, specifications, documentation, research, names, designs, methods, data, know-how, repositories, licenses, and other intellectual property created, authored, documented, or controlled by Mya P. Brown / The Commons Initiative before or independently of an outside engagement remain Background IP of their existing owner.

This includes, without limitation, Maya Node, SAGE Situated Companion, CERL, Commons Control Systems, related repositories, governance architectures, consent systems, audit systems, documentation, product names, and research artifacts.

Disclosure of Background IP does not by itself transfer ownership or create an implied license, commercialization right, sublicensing right, equity interest, partnership interest, or independent manufacturing right.

## 2. Need-to-know disclosure

Outside partners receive only the information reasonably necessary for the authorized scope. Unpublished source code, patent-relevant implementation details, private keys, credentials, and confidential product material should not be disclosed merely because a party is evaluating, quoting, or manufacturing a product.

## 3. Quote and evaluation do not authorize production

A request for quotation, estimate, DFM review, technical evaluation, or prototype discussion does not authorize:

- paid engineering,
- component purchasing,
- tooling fabrication,
- production quantities,
- commercial manufacture,
- resale,
- white-label use,
- independent derivative products,
- sublicensing or transfer to third parties.

Separate written authorization is required for each approved scope.

## 4. Project-created work must be allocated in writing

Before paid development begins, the written agreement should address ownership and delivery of project-specific work, including as applicable:

- CAD and enclosure files,
- schematics,
- PCB layouts and Gerbers,
- BOMs and sourcing files,
- firmware and source files,
- manufacturing drawings,
- test fixtures and test procedures,
- tooling and molds,
- production documentation,
- quality records.

The Commons Initiative's preferred commercial position is that project-specific work commissioned and paid for by The Commons Initiative is assigned to The Commons Initiative, while a contractor retains only its pre-existing general tools, know-how, and Background IP.

## 5. No independent exploitation

Unless a signed agreement expressly says otherwise, a contractor or manufacturer may not independently sell, license, reproduce, market, distribute, reverse-engineer for unrelated use, white-label, sublicense, or manufacture a Commons product or project-specific design for another customer.

## 6. Subcontractors

No subcontractor should receive rights broader than the prime contractor. Confidentiality and IP restrictions must flow down to any subcontractor receiving protected project information.

## 7. Tooling and production assets

Ownership, possession, storage, access, maintenance, transfer, and end-of-project disposition of customer-funded tooling, molds, fixtures, production files, and manufacturing documentation must be defined before payment.

## 8. No implied partnership or co-ownership

Engineering, manufacturing, consulting, evaluation, or collaboration does not by itself create equity, joint ownership, a partnership, or a license.

Patent inventorship is a legal determination based on actual inventive contribution and cannot be changed merely by labeling a relationship. Agreements should therefore address assignment obligations, confidentiality, and documentation of pre-existing inventions before substantive collaborative engineering begins.
