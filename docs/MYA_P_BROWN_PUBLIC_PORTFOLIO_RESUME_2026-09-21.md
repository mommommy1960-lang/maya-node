### Professional profile

Independent socio-technical systems accountability engineer, public-interest systems researcher, author, and founder whose work connects AI governance, institutional accountability, consent and revocation, public-system failure analysis, evidence preservation, and accessible technical communication. Since at least 2025, Brown has developed an open portfolio of governance frameworks, software prototypes, adversarial tests, technical papers, books, and public records designed around one operating principle: technical capability does not create legitimate authority.

Brown's practice is distinguished by turning lived institutional failure into inspectable system requirements. Her work asks who authorized an action, what limits apply, whether permission is current, how a person can refuse or appeal, whether intervention remains possible, and what evidence survives after failure. The portfolio includes eleven public GitHub repositories, DOI-registered research records, reader-facing governance materials, software test suites, public corrections, null-result preservation, and reviewer-facing evidence maps.

Brown's present public engineering and governance record dates to at least 2025, while the underlying idea archive was developed over many years. Her current practice is grounded in testable controls, documented limitations, human authority, and real-world institutional conditions.

## Leadership and working principles

- Human authority must remain meaningful, informed, and exercisable.
- Trust does not confer authority; capability does not confer sovereignty.
- Consequential systems require correction, refusal, escalation, appeal, and independent interruption.
- Accountability without the power to intervene is ceremonial.
- Claims must remain proportional to evidence.
- Negative results, revisions, and boundary failures belong in the public record.
- People affected by a system must be able to understand what happened and challenge it.
