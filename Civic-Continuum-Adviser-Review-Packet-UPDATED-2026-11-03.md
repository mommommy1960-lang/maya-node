## 4. Opportunity Discovery Canvas — updated discussion version

**Problem:** Small teams may use AI or automation without a clear owner, approval boundary, failure record or correction route. We need interviews to establish the frequency, consequences and priority of that problem.

**Buyer and user:** The first proposed buyer is the operations manager; staff who draft/review communications are users. Ask who controls the budget, who signs off and whether procurement is realistic at $150.

**Market size:** Not measured. Do not use broad AI adoption as proof of a reachable paying market. Start with a named list of potential interviewees and learn which share this exact problem.

**Alternatives:** Existing internal checklists, shared documents, technical writers and broader governance platforms. The October worksheet named Credo AI, Holistic AI and IBM watsonx.governance as examples; no fresh competitive feature or price comparison was performed for this packet.

**Proposed difference:** A small, inspectable deliverable linking the technical workflow to specific people who can approve, stop and correct it. Whether this is valuable enough to buy remains unproven.

**Low-risk experiment:** Produce one fictional sample; interview ten qualified buyers; aim for three scoped proposals and one paid pilot. These are proposed targets, not completed outcomes. Do not begin with live customer-sensitive data.

**Evidence worth paying for:** The buyer identifies a real recurring pain, uses the sample to resolve a concrete ownership/approval gap, agrees the deliverable and price, and accepts a paid engagement. Record actual payment only when confirmed. Measure delivery effort, revision burden, customer usefulness and whether the buyer requests further work or refers another team.
